1. **gnosis_paper_v4.docx** (authorship UNCLEAR — anonymous "Un-named",
   May 2026) — MEDIUM-HIGH. "Beyond the Self-Sealing Model: Epistemic
   Closure, Misspecification Detection, and the Limits of 'Gnosis' as
   Truth-Tracking Correction." Formal epistemology: inference-level
   epistemic closure (self-stabilizing architectures that filter evidence
   of their own insufficiency); the DISCRIMINATOR PROBLEM — how to
   distinguish genuine epistemic improvement from compelling-but-false
   model restructuring; the unreliable gradient problem (detecting
   misspecification doesn't guarantee the correction points at truth);
   tripartite partition of model-revision events (sealed / misdirected /
   truth-tracking); grounded via Watanabe's singular learning theory.
   Design target: "meta-misspecification detection that triggers
   structured doubt rather than assumed transcendence." Relevance: the
   discriminator problem IS the consolidation critic's core question —
   when the consolidation pass proposes merge/supersede, how do we know
   it's an improvement and not a compelling reconfiguration into a
   different error? "Structured doubt" is a design principle for
   consolidation proposals. Mine for the critic's adjudication criteria.

2. **Flexmem.pdf** (NOT Jay's — arXiv:2605.28773, Zhejiang/Alibaba/

   heterogeneous graph (semantic / episodic / procedural layers);
   three-stage evolution: initial connection formation → feedback-driven

   crystallization is the closest published analog to our consolidation
   pass; feedback-driven pruning and procedural-circuit distillation are
   donor mechanisms worth comparing against our rehearsal/consolidation
   design.
