THE NIGHT WINDOW
An original fragment for the Cozy Corner test library

The sound of rain changed as the hour went on. At first it tapped against the narrow panes; later it became a long, even noise on the slate roof, almost too continuous to hear. June had a book in her lap and a pencil in her hand, but she had been looking at the same paragraph for a while. It was not an especially difficult paragraph. She simply did not want to move on to the next one yet.

At the edge of the table stood a glass of water, partly full. A small bubble remained fixed against its inner wall. She watched it detach and rise. Nothing followed. She turned back to the book, read the first sentence again, and put the pencil down.

From below came the ticking of the kitchen clock. The sound reached her only when the rain weakened. Once or twice she mistook the slight movement of a branch against the window for someone approaching the door. There were no footsteps. She checked the latch and returned to her chair.

She knew she could make the evening busy. There was correspondence to answer, a cupboard to straighten, a picture to hang, and a loose button on a coat. Each could be finished and crossed off a list. Instead she watched the branch drag slowly across the dark pane. She did not call it rest. She did not need to call it anything.

When she finally closed the book, the page remained marked by the narrow ribbon. She would find it tomorrow if she wanted it. The water in the glass had become room temperature. The rain was almost over. She went downstairs without looking at the clock.
